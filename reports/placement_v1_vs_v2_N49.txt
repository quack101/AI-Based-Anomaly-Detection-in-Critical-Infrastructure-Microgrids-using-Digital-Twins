===============================================================
5-line summary
===============================================================
1. v1, re-run in memory through the same context-build path as
   select_meters.py, does NOT reproduce the saved 49-meter set: 4 of
   49 load names differ (s30c/s64b/s66c/s76a saved vs s12b/s29a/s2b/
   s84c fresh) -- a real divergence to flag, not a test that was run.
2. v1 and v2 agree on 36 of 49 metered buses (73%); v2's changes
   (farthest-first branch order + phase look-ahead) only move the
   other 13 meters, and the kW coverage and A/B/C phase split stay
   essentially tied across v1 (0.583, 24/14/17), v2 (0.595, 23/14/18)
   and random (0.586, ~22/16/16) -- these strategies differ in spatial
   spread, not in load or phase coverage.
3. v2 is a clear tail-coverage improvement over v1: mean hop-to-
   nearest-meter 0.85 vs 1.86, p95 2 vs 8, max 4 vs 12, over the 124
   topology buses reachable from any meter (bus 610 is structurally
   isolated -- zero edges in the collapsed topology graph -- for
   every strategy alike, and is excluded from these stats).
4. That gain is concentrated exactly where hybrid_v2.py's docstring
   says it should be -- the high-bus-number side of the feeder
   (64-114): v2 directly meters 18/51 of those buses vs v1's 13/51,
   mean hop 0.98 vs 3.37, max hop 3 vs 12.
5. The already-weak 80-83 area is covered within 1 hop by BOTH
   strategies (v1 is marginally better, by one bus: 82 is directly
   metered under v1, 1 hop away under v2) -- v2's improvement is
   additive on top of v1's existing 80-83 coverage, not a trade-off
   against it.
