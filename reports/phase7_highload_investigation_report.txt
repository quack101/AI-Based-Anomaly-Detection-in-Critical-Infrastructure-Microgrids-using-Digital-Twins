RES.md D10 Phase 7 HIGH-LOAD INVESTIGATION -- Report
======================================================================
Companion to all eight prior Phase 7 reports (state_estimator,
convergence_remediation, solver_instability_fix, accept_criterion_fix,
iteration_cap_test, node_demotion_test, scipy_trustconstr_test,

in the series, and the one that FINALLY FINDS, FIXES, AND VALIDATES
the dominant root cause -- a genuine, major turnaround after eight
reports of confirmed negative results.

HEADLINE RESULT: this task started as "diagnose why the bus-83
capacitor fix fails at high load" and found something much bigger.
Confirmed and fixed a SECOND, independent, far more consequential
bug (observability/estimator_model.py's REG_SIGMA_FLOOR) that had
been silently poisoning the WLS objective (J) across EVERY prior
report in this entire series -- not just the high-load case. Once
fixed, the HAND-ROLLED solver (estimation/state_estimator.py,
completely unmodified), which EVERY ONE of the five preceding reports
found converges 0% of the time, now converges on 76.7% (46/60) of the

permanently-red regression test (tests/test_state_estimator.py::
test_recovers_x_true_on_normal_timestamp) now PASSES, along with the
other 112 tests (113/113, up from 112/113 in every prior report).

This is a PARTIAL, not total, resolution: the WLS objective J(x_hat)
still does not reach the task's own target range (O(10^2-10^3)) --
it plateaus around 4.5-5.2 million, a genuine ~17,000x improvement
from 8.0e10 but not yet "sane". The remaining cause is traced to a
SPECIFIC, real AMI meter (M042, at bus 83 phase C -- the SAME bus the
capacitor fix already targets) whose tightly-calibrated reading the
solver cannot reconcile with the model's prediction at that
electrically-difficult node. A SEPARATE, newly-found bug in the
scipy trust-constr wrapper (estimation/state_estimator_scipy.py) is
also reported and left unresolved. RECOMMENDATION: COMMIT the sigma-
floor fix, the capacitor-bus demotion, and the switch-bus demotion
together (all three, as a package) to observability/zero_injection.py
and observability/estimator_model.py -- this reverses reports 5 and
6's "DO NOT COMMIT" verdicts, because those tests were run while the
sigma-floor bug was STILL silently active, confounding every
comparison made in this series up to and including report 8's own
60-point validation. Do NOT adopt estimation/state_estimator_scipy.py
as a replacement for the hand-rolled solver -- it has a separate,
unresolved bug (reported here, not fixed) and the hand-rolled solver
now performs substantially better once the REAL confound is removed.

4b. The REAL bug: REG_SIGMA_FLOOR poisoning J across the WHOLE series
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Verified directly (not inferred): at i=46, J(x_hat) = 80,005,329,085
(~8.0e10) -- for ~308 weighted measurements, this should be O(10^2-
10^3). Decomposed J row-by-row: TWO entries,
REGZI_83_A_Q and REGZI_83_B_Q (the bus-83 capacitor demotion's own new
rows), contributed 2 x 4.0e10 -- i.e. the ENTIRE objective, from just
2 of ~308 rows. Both had sigma=0.001 EXACTLY -- hitting
observability/estimator_model.py's `REG_SIGMA_FLOOR = 1e-3` constant.
Checked ALL reg_pseudo_entries: 84 of them (every switch-bus and
capacitor-bus demoted entry, z_value~0 in every case) sit EXACTLY at
this floor, giving each an effectively unbounded weight (W=1/sigma^2=
1e6) -- reintroducing the EXACT "near-zero-sigma masquerading as a
hard constraint" pathology Sha25's own Lagrangian design (reports/
phase7_state_estimator_report.txt Part A.2) exists to avoid.

ROOT CAUSE, STATED PLAINLY: REG_SIGMA_FLOOR=1e-3 was calibrated for
GENUINE regulator entries, where a near-zero empirical std across the
TRAIN split is a rare edge case (real regulator sigmas estimate in
the 75,000-1,700,000 range, confirmed). Once switch-bus and capacitor-
bus demotion were ADDED (reports 5 and this task's Step 3), the SAME
floor became the TYPICAL case for 84 new entries, because those are
DETERMINISTIC network elements (a fixed capacitor rating, a switch's
fixed topology across the train split) with GENUINELY ~0 true sample-
to-sample variance -- a different statistical situation the inherited
floor was never designed for.

NOT a z_value or h(x) magnitude bug: z_value~0 correctly matches
h_zi(x_true)=0 exactly (confirmed, re-used fact from reports/
phase7_state_estimator_report.txt Part D.2); h(x0)=-200 at bus 83 is a
physically sane nonlinear-iteration value (the capacitor's own per-
phase rating), not a units or sign error (the power-flow formula in
observability/jacobian.py's `_scatter_injection` was read directly and
found standard, symmetric between P and Q, with the SAME /1000 W->kW
conversion applied to both in `build_meter_pq_row`). ONLY the WEIGHT
was wrong.

FIX: raised REG_SIGMA_FLOOR from 1e-3 to 1.0 (kW/kvar) -- matching
this project's own established "fairly confident real sensor" scale
(sensors/registry's real AMI sigmas range 0.02-14.6 kW/kvar), not an
arbitrary pick. This is NOT "widening sigma to hide a bad z_value/h(x)"
(the thing this task explicitly warned against) -- z_value and h(x)
were already confirmed correct; only the CONFIDENCE assigned to a
correct value was wrong, and correcting a wrong weight is the intended
remedy this project's whole Sha25-based design already prescribes.

Result of this ONE fix (raising a single constant): J at i=46 dropped
from 8.0e10 to 4.68e6 -- a ~17,000x reduction -- confirmed via direct
recomputation, not just the solver's own self-report.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4d. The remaining J-floor, traced exactly: M042 is ALSO at bus 83
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Even at CONVERGED points, J plateaus at 4.5-5.2 million, not the
target O(10^2-10^3). Decomposed J at the converged x_hat for i=46:
the single largest contributor (3.7e6 of 4.59e6 total, ~81%) is
`M042_Q`, a REAL AMI meter (sigma=0.0997, genuinely tight, calibrated
hardware precision) with r=193.25 (barely moved from its flat-start
residual of 195.72, confirmed the solve could not meaningfully correct
it even though it DID correct other rows, e.g. HEAD_P/Q_*, to exactly
0). Checked sensors/registry directly: **M042 is placed at node=83,
phase=C, load_name=s83c** -- THE SAME BUS the capacitor fix already
targets. This is not a new, independent defect: it is the ALREADY-
DOCUMENTED bus-83 "strongly nonlinear (quadratic-in-V) self-
susceptance" difficulty (reports/phase7_state_estimator_report.txt
Part D.2, reports/phase7_constraint_feasibility_report.txt's root
cause), now visible through a REAL, tightly-weighted meter at that
bus's third phase (C) rather than through a pseudo-measurement at
phases A/B. The capacitor/switch demotion fixes correctly handle the
PSEUDO-measurement rows at bus 83's phases A/B; they cannot, by
construction, fix the REAL meter reading at phase C, which depends on
the SAME hard-to-linearize voltage at that node. This is confirmed
coherent with, not contradictory to, every prior finding about bus 83
in this series -- M042's residual is an ALREADY-EXPECTED, if newly-
quantified, consequence.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4e. scipy trust-constr: a separate, unresolved wrapper bug
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Checked directly: at i=46, scipy's trust-constr reports converged=True
(per this task's own loosened flag) with 24 "iterations" and 21
function evaluations -- but `result.x_v == state.x0_v` EXACTLY (bit-
for-bit, confirmed via np.array_equal). The optimizer evaluates the
function repeatedly but NEVER actually accepts a step away from the
scaled starting point y0, despite a nonzero gradient and a well-posed
(post-fix) objective. Re-tested with 6x the iteration budget and much
tighter gtol/xtol: IDENTICAL result (same J, same x). This is a real,
reproducible defect in HOW estimation/state_estimator_scipy.py's
NonlinearConstraint/minimize call is set up (most likely a scaling or
initial-trust-region-radius issue causing trust-constr's own first-
order optimality check to be satisfied spuriously at y0) -- NOT fixed
in this report (out of scope given the hand-rolled solver's much
better showing once the real confound was removed), but reported
plainly rather than left silently masked by the loosened `converged`
flag.
