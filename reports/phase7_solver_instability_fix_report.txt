1c. Testing the task's 4 candidate mechanisms against the evidence
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Wrong-block damping / Cholesky on an indefinite system: NO dense
    KKT block matrix exists in this code (Part A.1 of the original
    report explains why -- it was replaced by the null-space method
    specifically because forming H^T W H squares the condition
    number). No Cholesky factorisation appears anywhere in this
    solver; lstsq/SVD is used throughout.
  - Fixed vs adaptive damping magnitude: lm_lambda IS adaptive (accept
    -> decrease, reject -> increase, standard LM schedule) -- but only
    for `w`. This is CLOSE to correct but incomplete as a diagnosis on
    its own (see 1e below for why "just damp y_particular too" did
    not work).
  - Sign/assembly error: ruled out directly. cond(Cs) was measured at
    ~1.3e7 (validation/phase7_instability_repro.py) -- large but NOT
    the ~1e22 the module's own docstring attributes to A_reduced.
    y_particular's lstsq(rcond=None) and the explicit SVD used for Z
    give the SAME numerical rank (328, full row rank) at this specific
    point -- confirmed directly, not assumed -- ruling out a rank-
    cutoff mismatch between the two solves as the mechanism (this was
    my FIRST hypothesis, tested and FALSIFIED by the repro itself
    before any fix was attempted).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1d. The actual mechanism, confirmed by direct instrumentation
----------------------------------------------------------------------
validation/phase7_single_iteration_trace.py traces iteration 1 at the
exact failing point (i=46, delta_frac=0.001, x0 = x_true + 0.1%
independent per-node perturbation) end-to-end:

  max|dc| at x0 (constraint violation BEFORE any step):  22,153,629 kW
  y_particular (RAW, unclipped) max|dx| in actual units:  2,725 V / 0.289 rad
    -- 11.3x and 2.9x OVER the per-node trust-region cap respectively
  Z@w (tangential, first LM try, lambda=1.0) max|dx|:     1,481 V / 0.481 rad
    -- COMPARABLE IN SIZE to y_particular, despite lying EXACTLY in
       Cs's null space (Cs @ (s*Z@w) = 0 by construction -- confirmed
       algebraically, Z's columns span the null space Cs itself was
       built from) -- i.e. it can NEVER help correct the constraint,
       by definition, regardless of its magnitude.
  Combined step (y_particular + Z@w), frac=1.0:
    global step_ratio = 8.511   (shared clip ENGAGES)
    max|dx| actually applied: 240.2 V / 0.0473 rad (within cap, as intended)
    LINEAR-PREDICTED max|h_zi| after this step:    19,550,831 kW
    ACTUAL (nonlinear re-evaluated) max|h_zi|:      20,228,034 kW
    candidate_merit=4.340e19 < current_merit=4.937e19 -> ACCEPTED

Two things this directly proves:

  (i) LINEAR-PREDICTED (19.55M) and ACTUAL (20.23M) are close (~3.4%
      apart) -- this is NOT a nonlinearity / model-validity problem.
      The linearisation is a GOOD local model here; it correctly
      predicts that this clipped step will barely help. (Answers the
      review's explicit question directly: the problem does not live
      in "the step carries you into nonlinear territory the model
      can't see" -- it lives in the step being badly SIZED/ALLOCATED
      by a model that predicts its own effect correctly.)

  (ii) The arithmetic is exact and checkable: because Z@w is in Cs's
       null space, C @ (s*(y_particular+Z@w)/step_ratio) = dc /
       step_ratio EXACTLY (the Z@w term contributes zero to this
       product by construction). Predicted new |h_zi| = |dc| * (1 -
       1/step_ratio) = 22.15M * (1 - 1/8.511) = 19.55M -- MATCHES the
       instrumented value to 4 significant figures. This is not
       circumstantial; it is the exact mechanism, checked arithmetically:
       y_particular's otherwise-EXACT correction for dc is being
       divided by a shared step_ratio INFLATED by Z@w's own,
       constraint-irrelevant size, instead of by whatever y_particular
       ALONE would require. Over repeated iterations this produces a
       slow geometric decay (~(1-1/step_ratio) per iteration, ~88% of
       the previous residual in the traced case) that cannot reach the
       ~1 kvar threshold within a 30-iteration budget starting from a
       multi-million-kW violation -- 0.88^30 * 22.15M ~= 310,000,
       matching the ORDER OF MAGNITUDE of the actual 30-iteration
       outcome (560,763 kvar, phase7_circularity_diagnostic.py) to
       within the precision this rough first-iteration-only estimate
       should be expected to hold.

Verdict on the task's own framing: closest to candidate mechanism (1),
generalised -- NOT "damping applied to the wrong block of a KKT
matrix" literally (no such matrix exists post-null-space-
reformulation), but structurally the same disease: the ONE shared
scalar (step_ratio) that gates how much of the step survives conflates
TWO STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT components (the constraint-correcting
y_particular and the constraint-IRRELEVANT Z@w) into a single budget,
letting the irrelevant one's size dictate how much of the relevant
one's otherwise-exact correction is thrown away.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
1e. A genuinely new mechanism exposed while testing fix attempts: `w` itself is also inadequately damped
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The trace above shows Z@w's OWN magnitude (1,481 V) at lambda=1.0 (the
INITIAL, lightest damping level) is already far over the trust region
on its own. This means `w`'s existing LM damping -- the module
docstring's own claimed fix -- is not preventing an oversized
tangential step either; it only ever gets reined in by the SAME shared
step_ratio clip that also throttles y_particular. This was not stated
plainly in either prior report and is a real, previously-unstated
finding of this task's instrumentation, not a restatement of known
issues.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
2d. Why a mechanism-correct fix still failed -- the honest, unresolved part
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Attempt 3's failure, despite being arithmetically sound for the SINGLE
first-iteration mechanism Step 1 confirmed, is itself the most
important finding of this report: fixing the CONFIRMED first-iteration
allocation problem does not fix the 30-ITERATION TRAJECTORY. The most
likely explanation, consistent with all three attempts' results: the
ACCEPT/REJECT criterion (estimation/state_estimator.py's `if
cand_merit < current_merit`) is a crude binary test, not a calibrated
trust-region ratio (predicted vs actual reduction). It accepts ANY
step that improves the merit by ANY amount, however small, relative to
an ALREADY-catastrophic current_merit (~4.9e19 in the traced case,
dominated by LINESEARCH_CONSTRAINT_PENALTY=1e4 times a multi-million-
kW-squared residual). A better-ALLOCATED step (attempt 3) still gets
accepted at whatever reduction it happens to produce on THIS
iteration, but the resulting 30-STEP TRAJECTORY through a system this
sensitive (confirmed deterministic given identical inputs --
validation/_tmp_fix/determinism_check.log, three identical runs, bit-
identical output -- so trajectory differences between fix attempts
are NOT numerical noise, they are genuine, reproducible consequences of
each fix's different step shape compounding over 30 steps) can land in
a worse region than the unfixed code's trajectory, because "improves
this step's merit" says nothing about whether the NEXT 29 steps will
do so reliably. Root-causing THIS (the accept/reject criterion itself,
not the step-shaping mechanism Step 1 identified) is the right next
target, and is explicitly out of this report's "minimal diff" mandate.

STEP 4 -- COMMIT DECISION
======================================================================
DO NOT COMMIT. Explicitly, plainly: none of the three attempted fixes
satisfy Step 3a (no OOM blowup, low iteration count) or Step 3b (the
regression test passing). estimation/state_estimator.py is left
UNCHANGED from this task's starting state.

What still fails, and why: the catastrophic constraint-residual
blowup under a small perturbation near x_true (reports/
phase7_convergence_remediation_report.txt Step 3) is root-caused with
hard, checkable, arithmetic evidence (Step 1d above) to a shared trust-
region clip that conflates the constraint-correcting (normal) and
measurement-fit (tangential) components of the Newton step. THREE
textbook-motivated fixes targeting this exact, confirmed mechanism
were tried; the two that directly restructured the clip (attempts 2
and 3) made the targeted case WORSE (up to ~55x), not better, and the
one that left the clip structure alone and only damped y_particular
(attempt 1) produced a real but marginal (0-9%) improvement, nowhere
near sufficient. This pattern -- a mechanism-correct single-iteration
fix failing to improve, and sometimes harming, the full 30-iteration
outcome -- points to the ACCEPT/REJECT criterion itself (binary "merit
improves at all" rather than a calibrated trust-region ratio test) as
a deeper, compounding defect that this task's mandate (minimal diff to
the step-shaping mechanism) was not scoped to fix. A future pass should
target THAT criterion directly (e.g. requiring the ratio of actual to
PREDICTED merit reduction to exceed a threshold before accepting, the
standard trust-region acceptance test -- Nocedal & Wright ch.4) rather
than attempting a fourth variation on step allocation.

This finding REINFORCES, not overturns, reports/
phase7_convergence_remediation_report.txt's verdict: the original
"100% convergence, mean 1.00 iterations" claim stays RETRACTED. The
instability that claim's criterion was masking remains open.
