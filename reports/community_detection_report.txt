STAGE 1 -- EQUIVALENT ELECTRICAL DISTANCE (XuIoT26'S, verified)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  d^{mu,eta}_ij = |Y^{mu,eta}_ij|/|Y^{eta,eta}_jj| + |Y^{mu,eta}_ij|/|Y^{mu,mu}_ii|   eq.(4)-(5)
  d_ij = sum over mu,eta in {a,b,c} of d^{mu,eta}_ij                                  eq.(6)

  Y from OpenDSS SystemY (dss.YMatrix.getYsparse() + dss.Circuit.YNodeOrder()
  on the compiled, solved circuit) -- never a text parse.
  placement/electrical_distance.py.

  VERIFICATION against the paper's worked examples (eq. 33-38):

    Three-phase line, buses 1-7 (our unmodified IEEE-123):
      D_17 (row=phase of bus 1, col=phase of bus 7) =
        [[0.97, 0.36, 0.21],
         [0.29, 0.80, 0.22],
         [0.18, 0.23, 0.83]]
      -- EXACT match to the paper's matrix (2 decimals).
      sum d_17 = 4.101 (full precision) / 4.09 (summing the paper's
      rounded entries) -- exact match once you account for rounding
      accumulation. Paper states 4.09.
      diagonal share = 63.49% vs paper's 63.6% -- matches to rounding.

    Single-phase (phase b) line, buses 1-2:
      d^{bb}_12 = 1.3078 vs paper's 1.31 -- matches to rounding.
      All 8 other phase-pair entries = exactly 0.0, matching "all other
      entries 0."

  Our feeder is the unmodified IEEE-123; XuIoT26's is DG-modified, so
  exact bit-for-bit agreement was not expected and was not required --
  what was found is agreement to the paper's own stated rounding on
  both worked examples. The formula reading is confirmed correct.
  Tests: tests/test_electrical_distance.py (4 tests, all passing).

STAGE 2 -- COMMUNITY DETECTION (XuIoT26'S mechanism, OUR simplification)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  THREE GENUINE AMBIGUITIES IN THE SOURCE, as flagged in the task:

  (a) eq. (9)'s omega_ij term is never defined anywhere in the paper.
  (b) eq. (10) is written over A_ij and kappa_i*kappa_j (node-graph
      quantities) while the surrounding prose calls F (the edge graph
      built in eq. 7-9) the adjacency matrix being maximised over.
      Inconsistent as written.
  (c) The prose says smaller d_ij = tighter coupling = larger edge
      weight. The paper's OWN arithmetic disagrees: d_17=4.09
      (three-phase, tightly coupled) > d_12=1.31 (single-phase,
      loosely coupled) -- larger d for the TIGHTER connection. d_ij is
      a similarity, not a distance.

  RESOLUTION, given (a) and (b): standard weighted Louvain modularity
  maximisation runs directly on the NODE (bus) graph, A_ij = d_ij, not
  on the eq.(7)-(9) edge-graph transformation. XuIoT26 states that
  transformation's purpose is "scale equalization of each subregion" --
  a size-balancing refinement, not the core partitioning mechanism --
  so omitting it is a real, documented simplification of the
  algorithm, not an approximation of an unambiguous spec.
  placement/community_detection.py: standard multi-level Louvain
  (local-moving + aggregation), hand-implemented (no networkx /
  python-louvain in the environment), validated on a synthetic
  two-clique toy graph before running on the feeder.

  RESOLUTION of (c), tested empirically, not assumed
  (tests/test_community_detection.py::test_d_ij_weighting_beats_inverse_weighting):

    weight = d_ij:     modularity 0.9513, largest community 24/132 (18%)
    weight = 1/d_ij:   modularity 0.7415, largest community 53/132 (40%)

  weight=1/d_ij collapses into one dominant community absorbing 40% of
  the whole feeder plus small fragments -- not a sensible partition of
  a radial distribution feeder. weight=d_ij produces communities that
  visibly follow the feeder's actual laterals (see below). weight=d_ij
  is used, confirming the paper's arithmetic over its prose.

  RESULT at standard resolution (1.0, no tuning):
    12 raw communities. TWO are trivial: {"300_OPEN"} and {"94_OPEN"},
    the isolated dead-end stub buses from the switch-modelling quirk
    already flagged in model_verification_report.txt (FINDINGS #1) --
    genuinely electrically isolated, correctly left as singletons, not
    a partitioning failure.
    The other TEN are substantive and follow real feeder laterals:
      24: {67-79, 86-96}                (lower-right lateral)
      21: {97-114, 197, 300, 450}       (another lateral)
      19: {18-33, 135, 250, 25r}        (another lateral)
      18: {1-8, 12,13,15-17,34,149,150,152,150r}  (near source/head)
      18: {35-51, 151}                  (another lateral)
       8: {52-59}
       8: {60,62-66,160,160r}
       6: {80-85}
       5: {9,10,11,14,9r}
       3: {61,610,61s}

  COMPARISON to XuIoT26 Section III-B (their target: 4 subareas of
  ~30 nodes on their DG-modified IEEE-123; boundary nodes 18 between
  I/II, 67 between I/IV, 76 between III/IV):
    Our partition is FINER (10 substantive communities vs their 4) and
    NOT size-balanced (3-24 nodes vs their ~30 each). Most plausible
    cause: the omitted eq.(7)-(9) step's stated purpose IS size
    balancing -- without it, Louvain finds its unconstrained modularity
    optimum, which follows lateral boundaries tightly rather than
    grouping several laterals into one larger subarea.
    A resolution-parameter sweep (standard Reichardt-Bornholdt
    generalisation, not a new mechanism -- community_detection.py's
    `resolution` argument) was run to see whether a coarser partition
    recovers the paper's balance: resolution 0.2/0.1 gives 6
    communities sized [51,39,37,3,1,1] -- fewer, but MORE unbalanced,
    not less. No resolution value produced anything close to 4 balanced
    ~30-node communities; lower resolution just merges the three
    largest laterals into increasingly lopsided blobs while the small
    fragments persist regardless. This is further evidence that the
    balance comes specifically from the omitted step, not from a
    tunable modularity parameter -- so resolution=1.0 (standard, no
    arbitrary tuning) is what ships, and the divergence from the
    paper's balance is reported rather than forced away.
    Boundary nodes: 18 and 67 ARE boundary nodes in our partition
    (different community than at least one topological neighbour) --
    matches the paper. 76 is NOT a boundary node in our partition (all
    its neighbours are in its own community, 76-79/86-96) -- does not
    match. 2 of 3 named boundary nodes reproduce.
  Tests: tests/test_community_detection.py (10 tests covering weight
  choice, determinism, the pinned 12-community/size result, and the
  2/3 boundary-node match).

STAGE 3 -- METER SELECTION FROM COMMUNITIES (OURS -- no source)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  XuIoT26 partitions the feeder for distributed state-estimation data
  reconstruction, not for meter placement -- this stage has no paper to
  follow and is entirely our design, documented as such per your
  instruction (goes in the RES.md D8 traceability table as "ours").

  Algorithm: allocate the target_meters budget across communities
  proportional to each community's share of TOTAL CONNECTED kW
  (largest-remainder / Hare-Niemeyer apportionment -- exact integer sum,
  deterministic tie-break by community id, same proportional-kW
  reasoning HybridPlacement's phase-balance uses). Within each
  community, select its highest-kW loads first, one per bus, up to its
  allocation. If a community's allocation exceeds its available
  distinct load buses (small/load-free communities exist -- e.g. the
  two dead-end stubs have none), the shortfall tops up globally from
  the highest-kW remaining candidates across all communities, exactly
  like HybridPlacement's top-up pass (Step-3 defect #1).

  Verified: len(selected) == target_meters exactly across all 9
  sub-study counts (15/30/44/45/49/60/70/80/85); target_meters > 85
  raises; no duplicate buses; deterministic; allocation sums to target
  exactly (tested directly, including a proportionality check: 900kW
  vs 100kW communities at target=10 allocate 9/1). At target=49, the
  selection spans 9 of the 10 substantive communities (2 got zero
  budget, proportional to their small kW share) -- not degenerating
  into a single-community selection.

UPDATE -- MERGED (4-COMMUNITY) VARIANT (OURS, no XuIoT26 source)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Follow-up task: add a variant that agglomeratively merges the 12 raw
  communities down to a target count (default 4, matching XuIoT26's
  reported subarea count) by strongest inter-community edge weight (sum
  of d_ij across the boundary, merging the strongest-coupled pair each
  step). Both variants are kept selectable
  (CommunityDetectionPlacement(num_communities=None) for the raw
  12-community result, num_communities=4 for the merged one) -- the
  raw result is not replaced; it remains the faithful output of the
  equations we can implement, per your instruction.

  RESULT at target=4: sizes [93, 37, 1, 1] -- NOT balanced (the paper's
  reported subareas are ~30 nodes each). The two size-1 communities are
  the same isolated dead-end switch stubs (300_OPEN, 94_OPEN) from
  FINDINGS #1 of model_verification_report.txt -- correctly untouched,
  since they share no edge with anything to merge into. Of the 93+37
  real nodes, one community absorbs 93 (70% of the whole feeder).

  WHY: greedy merge-by-total-edge-weight is size-blind -- it keeps
  attaching the next community to whichever existing community already
  has the largest AGGREGATE boundary weight, which tends to be the
  already-largest community (more member nodes -> more boundary edges
  -> more accumulated weight), so growth snowballs into one dominant
  cluster rather than balancing. This is a real and reportable
  limitation of the specified merge heuristic, not a bug -- the
  algorithm was implemented exactly as specified (sum of d_ij across
  the boundary, strongest pair each step, no size normalisation).

  BOUNDARY NODES in the merged variant:
    node 18: boundary  -- YES (same as the raw 12-community result)
    node 67: boundary  -- NO  (REGRESSION -- was a boundary node in the
                                raw 12-community result; merging put it
                                and its neighbour 97 in the same
                                surviving giant community)
    node 76: boundary  -- NO  (still does not reproduce -- unchanged
                                from the raw 12-community result)

  So merging to match XuIoT26's reported COUNT (4) does NOT bring the
  boundary-node comparison closer to their result -- it goes from 2/3
  matching (raw 12-community: 18, 67) to 1/3 matching (merged: 18 only).
  Getting the count right and getting the boundaries right are
  different problems, and this heuristic solves neither well beyond
  the count itself. Both facts -- the count now matches, the boundary
  match got WORSE -- are reported together, not cherry-picked.

  Tests: tests/test_community_detection.py (5 new tests: exact target
  count, determinism, monotonic non-increase, the pinned [93,37,1,1]/
  boundary-node result, both variants independently selectable and
  distinct).

normalisation) is exactly what was asked for and is exactly why it
snowballs -- a size-normalised variant (e.g. weight / (size_i * size_j))
would likely balance better but was not requested and would be a
further, separate adaptation beyond what's documented here. Feeds
directly into Part B's sweep as one of five strategies.
