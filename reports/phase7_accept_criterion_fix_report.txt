WHY A THEORETICALLY SOUND FIX STILL FAILED -- ANALYSIS
======================================================================
This is now the FOURTH independently-motivated, textbook-correct fix
(three step-construction attempts in the prior report, this accept-
criterion attempt) to fail to resolve, or to actively worsen, the
targeted instability. A pattern this consistent is itself informative.

Mechanically: at the FIRST outer iteration of the traced repro point,
the prior report's instrumentation already showed the LINEAR
prediction and the ACTUAL nonlinear outcome AGREE closely (~3.4%
apart, Step 1d of that report) -- meaning for at least that first
step, ratio should come out close to 1.0 (numerator and denominator
both reflecting the same ~12% real reduction), comfortably above
ETA_ACCEPT=0.1, and ALSO above ETA_GROW=0.75 -- triggering the "strong
accept, shrink lambda further/faster" branch. That extra lambda
decrease is the most likely source of the regression: it makes `w`
LESS damped, SOONER, across the 30-iteration trajectory than the
original code's single-factor decrease would have -- and reports/
phase7_solver_instability_fix_report.txt Step 1e already found `w`
itself (even at the ORIGINAL, single-factor-decreasing lambda
schedule) was producing a tangential step (~1,481 V) comparably large
to y_particular's own (~2,725 V) at lambda=1.0 -- i.e. `w` is ALREADY
inadequately damped at the ORIGINAL schedule's slowest decrease rate;
decreasing lambda FASTER on strong accepts (this fix's own "optional"
feature) plausibly makes that pre-existing problem worse, sooner, on a
DIFFERENT subset of the 30 iterations than the baseline trajectory
hits it -- consistent with the determinism check in the prior report
(identical code, identical inputs, identical output every time) that
established these differences are genuine, reproducible consequences
of each fix's changed trajectory, not noise.

The honest conclusion: FOUR theoretically well-motivated interventions
-- symmetric LM damping, two different trust-region-clip restructurings,
and now a calibrated ratio-based accept test -- have each targeted a
real, confirmed defect in isolation (Step 1 of the prior report's
diagnosis is not in question; this report's own Step 1 citation of the
binary accept test is also not in question) and each has failed to
improve, or has worsened, the actual 30-ITERATION OUTCOME at the
specific repro point tested. This is no longer well-explained by any
ONE single mechanism; it indicates the full dynamical system (30
chained Gauss-Newton iterations, each with its own nested LM/frac
retry loop, interacting with a genuinely multi-modal near-zero-
impedance-branch topology -- Part B of the original report already
found AT LEAST 15 regulator-adjacent nodes behaving unreliably, and
this task's Step 1 found at least as many additional tie-switch nodes,
e.g. ZI_300_open_*, ZI_149_*, ZI_151_*, with similarly extreme
sensitivity) is too sensitive to single-mechanism patches for further
blind iteration to be a responsible use of additional attempts. Each
of the four fixes was well-reasoned, each was tested honestly, and
each failed -- that is itself the finding this report delivers,
not a gap in effort.
